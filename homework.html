My 20 Irregular Nouns (Rule 6)

At the end of Lesson 3 you asked me to prepare 10 examples for Rule 6. I decided to prepare 20 examples with full speaking sentences so we can practice them together.

Hi Eliza! In Lesson 3 you taught me the six rules for making singular nouns plural. Rule 6 was about irregular nouns that change completely or follow unique patterns.

I found this topic very interesting because for years I thought English just added "-s" to everything. I grouped these 20 words by their logical patterns and wrote a full sentence for each one about real life, work, travel, and Bali.

Part 1

Ten core irregular nouns (people, body & nature)

Essential Everyday Words

These are the most important irregular words in English. I prepared full sentences so I can read them out loud to you.

1 man → 2 men /mæn/ → /mɛn/
Sentence: Two men in our team are senior product designers.
1 woman → 2 women /ˈwʊm.ən/ → /ˈwɪm.ɪn/
Sentence: Three women work on the new user interface project.
1 child → 2 children /tʃaɪld/ → /ˈtʃɪl.drən/
Sentence: My friends have two children, a boy and a girl.
1 person → many people /ˈpɜː.sən/ → /ˈpiː.pəl/
Sentence: There are many creative people living and working in Bali.
1 tooth → all teeth /tuːθ/ → /tiːθ/
Sentence: I always brush my teeth in the morning and before sleep.
1 foot → 2 feet /fʊt/ → /fiːt/
Sentence: My feet hurt after walking ten kilometers in the city.
1 mouse → 2 mice /maʊs/ → /maɪs/
Sentence: I don't like mice, but our cat loves chasing them.
1 life → many lives /laɪf/ → /laɪvz/
Sentence: People say that cats have nine lives.
1 sheep → 10 sheep no change: /ʃiːp/
Sentence: When I traveled to the mountains, I saw a lot of white sheep.
1 fish → many fish no change: /fɪʃ/
Sentence: There are thousands of colorful fish near the coral reef.

Part 2

Ten unique irregular nouns (new patterns)

Not discussed in Lesson 3

Here are 10 extra words grouped by 5 interesting phonetic and linguistic patterns:

Pattern A: The -f / -fe → -ves voice shift

The hard "F" sound turns into a soft "V" sound to make pronunciation smoother.

1 knife → 2 knives /naɪf/ → /naɪvz/ (k is silent)
Sentence: Could you please give me two sharp knives for cooking?
1 shelf → 3 shelves /ʃɛlf/ → /ʃɛlvz/
Sentence: I have three wooden shelves on the wall for my books and equipment.
1 leaf → many leaves /liːf/ → /liːvz/
Sentence: In Bali you can see huge green palm leaves everywhere.
1 half → 2 halves /hæf/ → /hævz/ (l is silent)
Sentence: Please cut the pizza into two equal halves.
1 thief → 2 thieves /θiːf/ → /θiːvz/
Sentence: The security camera recorded two thieves at night.

Pattern B: Vowel mutation (oo → ee)

Similar to tooth → teeth and foot → feet.

1 goose → 5 geese /ɡuːs/ → /ɡiːs/
Sentence: A flock of wild geese flew across the sky.

Pattern C: Modern zero plurals (never change)

Words that stay 100% identical in singular and plural.

1 series → 3 series /ˈsɪə.riːz/ (always ends with s)
Sentence: I watched two great detective series on Netflix recently.
1 aircraft → 4 aircraft /ˈeə.krɑːft/
Sentence: Several commercial aircraft landed at the international airport.

Pattern D: The ancient Germanic -en suffix

The only word in English beside "children" that still keeps the old "-en" plural.

1 ox → 2 oxen /ɒks/ → /ˈɒk.sən/
Sentence: Farmers in traditional villages still work with oxen.

Pattern E: Greek & Latin loan words (-is → -es)

Common in business, finance, and technology news.

1 crisis → many crises /ˈkraɪ.sɪs/ → /ˈkraɪ.siːz/
Sentence: Strong businesses know how to navigate global economic crises.

How to check in Lesson 4

Our 5-minute homework check plan

How I would like us to review this:

  1. Speaking Drill (3 minutes): You say the singular noun (e.g., "knife"), and I will immediately reply with my full sentence in the plural form ("Could you please give me two sharp knives?").
  2. Pronunciation Check (2 minutes): Please check my pronunciation of tricky words, especially women /ˈwɪm.ɪn/ and crises /ˈkraɪ.siːz/.
  3. Immediate Transition: After these 5 minutes, we move directly to our main target: am / is / are and fixing the "it's vs is" mistake!
